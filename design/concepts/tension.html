Tension.

SIX's home already uses plasma clay: cards closer than 40 px flow into one surface. These studies add what happens in between: surfaces that stretch, pull and let go, so the link between two things, and the moment it breaks, is something you can see. Every stage on this page is live. Drag, click and pull.

Six studies

Where tension would earn its place in SIX

Each study takes one SIX surface where a relationship is currently invisible, or a moment of commitment happens too abruptly, and shows it with an elastic surface instead.

Home · stacks

Pull a card out of a stack

The top card stays joined to the stack by a neck that thins as you pull. Past 150 px it lets go with a small recoil, and that release is the moment the card leaves the stack. Let go sooner and it springs back in.

In SIX
Replaces today's instant pull-out on home stacks; the cards behind lean after the one you're pulling.
Try
Pull the top card slowly, then quickly; drop it inside and outside the snap distance.
Pip · the notch

Pip reaches for what it changes

When Pip acts on a window, the notch drops a tendril to it and holds on while the window moves, so you see exactly what Pip is touching. When it's done, the tendril thins, lets go and draws back into the notch, and the notch leaves.

In SIX
The notch while Pip opens, snaps or closes windows, or edits a widget. One tether at a time.
Try
Ask Pip to snap Files to the left.
Control Center

Related controls fuse

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are both radios, so when both are on they flow into one body. Airplane mode pulls them apart. Pull a slider's thumb off its track and the track stretches after it like a membrane, then snaps back.

In SIX
Control Center tiles and sliders; the same rule fuses Do Not Disturb with Focus.
Try
Turn on Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, then Airplane mode. Drag a slider up or down as well as along.
The Pip app

Answers grow out of questions

Your message buds off the composer and rises into place. While Pip works, a drop travels out of your message; it swells into Pip's answer, which stays linked to the question by a hairline while it's the latest exchange.

In SIX
The Pip app and the notch's voice panel; review cards hang from the answer that raised them.
Try
Ask Pip a few times and watch older exchanges let go.
Files · the Bin

Files hold on, the Bin reaches out

A file stays tethered to its folder until you clearly mean to move it. Near the Bin, the Bin bulges toward it; drop it in and it's swallowed with a gulp. Put back buds it out of the Bin and carries it home.

In SIX
Dragging in Files and to the dock's Bin. The tether makes an accidental drag obviously harmless.
Try
Drag a file to the Bin, then put it back.
Right-click menus

Menus bud from where you click

A menu grows out of the pointer, joined to it until it settles; a submenu buds from its row. Choosing an item draws the menu back into the point it came from, so it's clear what you dismissed and where.

In SIX
Every right-click menu and the dock's menus (which bud upward).
Try
Click anywhere, hover Wallpaper, choose something.
Rules

How tension behaves in SIX

  1. Tension means a real relationship.A neck only ever joins things that belong together (a card and its stack, Pip and what it's changing, a reply and its question). Nothing stretches for decoration.
  2. Necks thin with distance, then snap.Thickness falls off as the gap grows; at the snap distance it lets go. The snap is the commit: before it, letting go cancels.
  3. Content never stretches.Text, icons and images stay rigid on top of the surface. Only the glass bends.
  4. Springs, not wobble.Moves use SIX's precise spring; a snap or a landing gets one detent, with a recoil under 4 %. Nothing jiggles on its own.
  5. One tether per thing.An object is joined to at most one other at a time, and budding goes at most two levels deep (menu, submenu).
  6. Every stretch has a plain alternative.Each gesture is also a menu item, a button or a key. Under Reduce motion, necks are drawn still and a snap is instant.

Recommendation

What to build into the prototype first

  1. Pull a card out of a stack. It fixes a real problem (pulling a card out today is instant and easy to do by accident) and it extends the plasma clay home already has, so it costs little.
  2. Pip reaches for what it changes. It makes Pip's actions legible: you see which window Pip is touching before it moves it. That's a trust feature, not decoration.
  3. Related controls fuse. A small, contained place to prove the merge rule with real state.
  4. Files hold on, the Bin reaches out. Valuable, but it needs drag and drop between Files and the dock first.
  5. Answers grow out of questions, and menus bud. Polish once the first three have been tested with people; both run often, so they need to stay quick.
Concept studies for SIX's Soft Matter design language, after the Tensionmorphism reference (stretchable surfaces, visual relationships, dynamic feedback). Surfaces are drawn on the GPU as satin glass, mostly frosted with a hint of what's behind (smooth-union distance fields with a little refraction, a bevel, rim light and soft shadow), and move on SIX's springs. All six studies are now built into the SIX prototype, following the rules and tokens above.
