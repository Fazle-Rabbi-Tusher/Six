Soft Matter

A familiar Windows map rendered in soft materials: clay you press, glass that floats, paper you work on, and plasma clay that melts together on the home screen, where Pip, the assistant, waits for you.

    Familiar map, fresh materials. Start bottom-left, controls top-right, Windows shortcuts. We change how it feels, not where things are.

Plasma clay

Home widgets and Pip’s voice captions are one liquid: rounded shapes joined with a smooth union, so cards closer than the blend distance (40 px) melt into each other. The surface bends the wallpaper behind it, frosts it, lights it like clay from the top left, and wears an iridescent rim. Viscosity sets how cards follow your hand: water wobbles, honey drags, solid doesn't move at all. Try it in Settings › Appearance.

The SIX assistant

Pip lives on the home screen

Home is voice first: press the mic and talk, or say “Hey Pip”. Pip answers aloud and in a caption, opens and arranges windows, and changes settings; the written conversation is in the Pip app at the top right. Pip has four looks (Settings › Pip & voice › Look): Bloom, the default; Halo; Ripple; and the dotted sphere. Every state moves Pip's colour differently, never its shape. Pick one to set Pip everywhere.

Bloom

A soft ball of warm colour: orange and red flowing over violet, with a pink haze lighting the air around it and a fine film grain, so it reads as a material and not a gradient. It is shaded as a matte ball, lit from the side Pip is looking at, and the only colour in an otherwise graphite and silver system. Thinking stirs it and sends a spark across; listening ripples it; working fills it like a glass of water as the task progresses; Complete warms it green once; Failed cools it red.

Halo

A ring of cold light spinning about its vertical axis, caught over a short exposure, so it reads as a 3D shell: brightest at the top and bottom where you see the ring edge-on, with long-exposure streaks running through the light. Its outline is always a circle, and it has no backing: on a light page it is drawn as blue ink. Working lights it from the bottom up as the task progresses; searching and waiting send a bright knot round it.

Ripple

A terraced pool seen from above, from the teal rings it is modelled on: steps of colour falling to a dark centre, each lip lit on the side toward the light and shading the step below. The deeper rings shift further as Pip looks around, so it reads in depth, and the rings flow slowly outward like ripples. Listening sends waves through it; thinking winds the rings into a spiral; working fills it from the centre out.

Personality

  • 1
    Pip looks at you. The light on Bloom and the tilt of Halo turn toward the pointer.
  • 2
    Pip blinks. While idle, Pip squashes for a moment every few seconds, then springs back.
  • 3
    Pip is water. Poke it and it squashes; drag it across home and it stretches the way it's moving and wobbles to a stop.
  • 4
    Pip reacts. It hops when a task completes, shakes its head when something fails, nods when it asks you something, and says hello with a hop when it first appears.
  • 5
    Pip is curious. Bring the pointer close and it leans in, looks harder and brightens; start typing and it glances at the field.
  • 6
    Pip gets drowsy. Left alone on home for a while it dims, breathes slower and looks down; move the mouse and it wakes with a little hop.

The dotted look

The third look, and the orb in the gallery below: a sphere of dots whose light moves differently in each state, so it reads at 18 px and in grayscale.

The orb's motion is ported from Thinking Orbs by Yogesh (MIT): one shared clock per look, so every orb in a state moves in step, and dots that fade toward the back instead of leaving a grey haze.

Voice

  • 1
    Press to talk, or say “Hey Pip”. The mic in the menu bar, clicking Pip, holding Space or Six + Space. “Hey Pip” is a choice in Control Center, detected on this device. Amber mic = hearing you; a green dot = waiting for “Hey Pip”.
  • 2
    See what Pip heard. The live transcript forms in the caption under Pip, so a wrong word is caught before anything runs.
  • 3
    Hear the answer, read it too. Every spoken reply is also a caption and a message in the Pip app. Changes to files wait for your “yes”.
