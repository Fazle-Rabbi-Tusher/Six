Soft Matter

A familiar Windows map rendered in soft materials: clay you press, glass that floats, paper you work on, and plasma clay that melts together on the home screen, where Pip, the assistant, waits for you.

Materials

Pick by role, never by taste

Clay objects are small and filled, so their colour carries contrast. Glass only floats over content and never drops below 78 % opacity. Paper is where text lives.

Plasma clay

Home cards and the composer are one liquid: rounded shapes joined with a smooth union, so cards closer than the blend distance (40 px) melt into each other. The surface bends the wallpaper behind it, frosts it, lights it like clay from the top left, and wears an iridescent rim. Viscosity sets how cards follow your hand: water wobbles, honey drags, solid doesn't move at all. Try it in Quick Settings.

Rules

Do put text on glass only above a 78 % fill; turn on Reduce transparency and check it still works.
Don't show state with shadow alone. Toggled-on clay changes fill, glyph and label.

The SIX assistant

Pip lives on the home screen

Home is a conversation. Type or talk; Pip answers, opens and arranges windows, and changes settings. Every state changes shape and motion, not just colour. Pick one to set Pip everywhere.

The small orb

In the menu bar, the dock, task cards and message labels Pip becomes a dotted sphere. Each state moves its light differently, so it reads at 18 px and in grayscale.

Voice

  • 1
    Tap the mic, or hold Six + Space. No always-on wake word by default. The mic glyph and Pip's flat, rippling rings show it is listening.
  • 2
    See what Pip heard. The live transcript sits in the composer, so a wrong word is caught before anything runs.
  • 3
    Hear the answer, read it too. Spoken replies always appear in the thread. Tap the mic again to interrupt.
